Data & Methods — the science behind MOUSUMI

MOUSUMI is built for the NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge “Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data.” It is a decision-support tool that combines NASA Earth observations with local soil information, crop characteristics and farmer priorities to explore rotation strategies that strengthen soil health and adapt farms to changing conditions — exactly as the challenge asks.

1. NASA data sources

  • NASA POWER v8 (Agroclimatology) — primary data source. Monthly (7 parameters, 1981–2025) and daily (rainfall, Tmax) products for all 64 district centroids, retrieved live from the official POWER API: https://power.larc.nasa.gov (Stackhouse et al., NASA Langley Research Center).
  • Parameters used: PRECTOTCORR (corrected precipitation), T2M (2 m air temperature), T2M_MAX (daily), RH2M (relative humidity), WS2M (wind speed), ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN (surface solar radiation).
  • Complementary NASA missions documented for extension (see README): GPM IMERG rainfall, SMAP soil moisture, MODIS/VIIRS vegetation & temperature, NASA Earthdata/Worldview — for validation and richer monitoring as the tool grows.

2. Water balance — FAO-56

Reference evapotranspiration is computed with the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation (Allen et al. 1998) at monthly step from NASA POWER inputs. Monthly mean daily-maximum temperature is computed from the POWER daily product (we verified empirically that POWER's monthly T2M_MAX is a monthly extreme, not a mean — see audit below). Monthly mean daily-minimum temperature is estimated as Tmin ≈ 2·T2M − Tmax (documented approximation). Crop water use is ETc = Kc × ETo with FAO-56 crop coefficients; effective rainfall uses the FAO AGLW monthly method (Pe = 0.6P if P<70 mm, else 0.8P−24).

ETo = [0.408·Δ·(Rn − G) + γ·(900/(T+273))·u₂·(eₛ − eₐ)] / [Δ + γ·(1 + 0.34·u₂)]

3. Soil salinity — local information + FAO-29 science

District baseline salinities are compiled from the SRDI (Soil Resource Development Institute, Bangladesh) salinity survey as reported in Dasgupta et al. (2015, World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 7140). Users can — and should — replace them with their own soil-test values (input box in the Rotation Explorer). Crop salt tolerance follows the Maas-Hoffman piecewise model: relative yield = 100% up to the threshold ECt, then falls by slope b% per dS/m (Ayers & Westcot 1985, FAO I&D Paper 29; BRRI variety documentation for salt-tolerant rice such as BRRI dhan67, 8–10 dS/m at vegetative stage).

Yrel = 100 − b·(ECe − ECt)  (when ECe > ECt)

4. Trend analysis — Mann-Kendall + Theil-Sen

Recent tendencies (2011–2025) are tested with the non-parametric Mann-Kendall test (tie-corrected) and Theil-Sen slope estimator, reported with p-values. Monsoon onset is the first day after 25 May with ≥60 mm accumulated in 10 days AND ≥40 mm in the following 10 days (persistence filter against pre-monsoon thunderstorms); withdrawal is the last day after 15 Sep in a 10-day window with ≥60 mm.

5. Data audit — why we don't claim 45-year trends

Scientific integrity note. Our homogeneity audit of NASA POWER v8 over Bangladesh reveals large multi-decadal discontinuities: decadal mean rainfall for the Sylhet grid cell roughly doubles from 1981–90 to 2021–25, and 1980s daily Tmax is warm-biased by ~2–3 °C versus gauge climatology. These are known limitations of reanalysis-based products in the tropics. Consequences: (1) current-condition modelling uses the recent 10-year window (2016–2025); (2) tendencies are computed over 2011–2025 and labelled indicative; (3) long-term change direction is cited from peer-reviewed literature (IPCC AR6; Bangladesh climate studies; World Bank salinity projections) rather than extrapolated from an inhomogeneous reanalysis; (4) the full record stays visible for transparency. We believe a trustworthy advisory tool must audit its data — this table is shown for every district in the District Dashboard.

6. Rotation engine

Feasible annual sequences are assembled from Bangladesh's three cropping seasons (Kharif-2 Jul–Oct, Rabi Nov–Apr, Kharif-1 Apr–Jun) across 15 crops (rice varieties incl. salt-tolerant BRRI dhan67, wheat, maize, potato, mustard, lentil, chickpea, onion, sunflower, groundnut, sesame, mungbean, jute, fallow). Each plan is scored on irrigation need (mm), salinity-adjusted expected yield and gross margin, heat risk in sensitive windows, soil-health effects (legume credit, continuous-cereal penalty), and family diversity. Farmer-priority sliders re-weight the score live. Gross margins are indicative 2023–25 farm-gate estimates and are editable — local calibration is a feature, not an admission of weakness.

7. Assumptions & limitations (honest list)

  • POWER grid resolution is 0.5° × 0.625° (~50–60 km): neighbouring districts in one cell share values; district-level precision comes from user soil inputs, not the grid.
  • Salinity envelope: rabi peak ≈ 1.35 × baseline, monsoon-leached ≈ 0.55 × baseline — a first-order seasonalisation of the salt cycle; full salt-transport modelling is future work.
  • Gross margins are indicative and location-sensitive; users should calibrate with local DAE/BBS figures.
  • Indicative salinity thresholds (flagged in the crop table) apply where FAO-29 does not tabulate a crop; users can replace any value.
  • MOUSUMI is a planning-support tool for exploration and education, not a substitute for professional agronomic advice.
